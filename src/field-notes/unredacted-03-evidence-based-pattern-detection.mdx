NoAI exists to stop sensitive information from being sent out unnecessarily. If NoAI had to send a document to an LLM in order to decide what to redact, the sensitive information would already have left the user's device. That would defeat the whole point.

But if there is no LLM behind the redaction, how does NoAI know what to redact?

The answer is what we call **Evidence-Based Pattern Detection**.

## The Basic Idea

NoAI uses plain deterministic code, what programmers call rule-based pattern matching. It is made of several parts: regular expressions, validation logic, context rules, and carefully designed exceptions.

NoAI does not understand your document, and it does not attempt to. It works more like a metal detector for text: it mechanically reacts when a piece of text has a recognized shape, label, position, or mathematical fingerprint.

A pattern alone is not always enough. `Limited` may be part of a company name, or it may just be an ordinary word. A number may be a bank account, a page number, a clause number, or a quantity. NoAI tries to redact only when the surrounding evidence gives the pattern enough support.

## How It Works

Evidence-Based Pattern Detection takes many forms. NoAI's ruleset combines many small rule checks to detect sensitive information in different contexts. Here are a few illustrative examples.

### 1. Distinctive Shape

> Please email ==marcus.ivers@example.com== by Friday.

NoAI does not need to know who Marcus is. The string has the shape of an email address: a local part, an `@` sign, a domain, and a suffix. That is enough for a direct rule to catch it.

### 2. Label Plus Context

> Buyer: ==Northwind Solar Holdings Limited==

`Northwind Solar` by itself could be a project, a heading, a party, or nothing sensitive at all. But when it appears after `Buyer:` and ends with `Limited`, the evidence is stronger. The label and the company suffix trip the rule together.

### 3. Mathematical Fingerprint

> IBAN: ==GB82 WEST 1234 5698 7654 32==

Some identifiers can be checked instead of merely guessed. An IBAN has a prescribed format and a checksum. NoAI can run that validation locally in the browser. If the checksum fails, the text should not be treated the same way as a real account identifier.

### 4. Repeated Reference

> Signed by ==Priya Sloane==.

Later,

> ==Sloane== confirmed the payment schedule.

NoAI may first catch the full name where the evidence is strong, such as a signature or party context. Once that identity is established, a later short reference can also be caught if the surrounding text supports it.

## In Practice

This is an example of the kind of mixed text that appears in ordinary transactional work.

> From: ==completion@stonepier.example==
> Subject: ==Project Keel completion==
> Attention: ==Mr. Tyler Hale==
> Seller: ==Blue Harbor Ventures Ltd== confirms that ==US$2,450,000== is due by ==14 October 2026== under ==Payment Reference: PAY-2026-5512-CUST2099==. Dear ==Mr. Hale==, please circulate the signed board minutes after receipt.

Processed by NoAI's current Balanced ruleset, it becomes:

> From: EMAIL_001
> Subject: PROJECT_OR_ISSUE_001
> Attention: PERSON_001
> Seller: ORG_001 confirms that AMOUNT_001 is due by DATE_001 under BUSINESS_ID_001. Dear PERSON_001, please circulate the signed board minutes after receipt.

The email is caught by shape. The subject line is treated as a matter or project reference because of its label. The attention line gives enough structure for a person redaction. The seller is caught by company suffix. The amount and date have recognizable formats. The payment reference is caught because it is introduced by a specific label.

None of these signals requires NoAI to understand the email.

## Fully Local

NoAI conducts detection, redaction, and restoration locally in the browser running on your device. No document is uploaded for redaction. No LLM is asked to read it.

NoAI is deliberately a machine of deterministic rules, not an AI tool. It's in the name. It's not as 'smart' as LLMs, but that's the whole point.
